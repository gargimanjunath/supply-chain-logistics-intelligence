1. BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
Between January 2015 and September 2017 (the complete-data window), the
business processed 65,752 distinct orders across 384,079 units, generating
$36.78M in total revenue at an average order value of $559.45. Delivery
performance is the standout concern: only 45.17% of line items arrived
on time, meaning 54.83% were late - a majority-late operation. Average
actual delivery time sits at 3.5 days. Cancellations are a minor factor
at 2.05% of orders. Fishing is the top-performing category (18.84% of
revenue) and Western Europe the top region (16.02% of revenue).

2. KEY DRIVERS OF LATE DELIVERY (statistically validated)
A one-way ANOVA confirms that shipping mode has a highly significant
relationship with actual delivery time (F=38,762.4, p<0.001) - this is
not noise, it is the primary lever available to operations. By contrast,
a chi-square test found no significant association between customer
segment and late-delivery risk (chi2=1.03, p=0.60) - segment-targeted
delivery interventions would not be an effective lever. The average
shipping delay (actual vs. scheduled) is 0.57 days (95% CI: 0.559-0.573),
a tight, well-estimated figure worth tracking as a standing KPI.

3. DEMAND OUTLOOK (next 6 months)
The Holt-Winters model (fit on 27 months of complete data, validated on a
held-out 6-month test window) reduced forecast error by 29.5% versus a
naive baseline (MAE 581.5 vs. 825.3 units/month). Projected monthly unit
demand for the next six periods ranges from roughly 10,000 to 11,100
units, with no sustained growth or decline signal - demand is expected
to remain broadly flat near recent levels. Note: three trailing months
in the raw data (Nov 2017-Jan 2018) were excluded from training as a
confirmed data-truncation artifact, not a genuine demand collapse.

4. ML MODEL RECOMMENDATION
Four classifiers were compared for predicting late delivery using only
information available at order time (shipping mode, scheduled duration,
region, category, order value, customer segment, timing - with no
post-fulfillment fields, to prevent leakage). Random Forest performed
best (ROC-AUC ~0.75) and is recommended for deployment as an early-warning
score at order-entry time.

5. RECOMMENDATIONS
(1) Since shipping mode is the dominant, statistically validated driver of
delivery delay, prioritize a mode-by-mode SLA review - starting with
whichever modes show the largest actual-vs-scheduled gap - rather than
broad, undifferentiated logistics investment.
(2) Deploy the late-delivery risk score at order entry so at-risk orders
(flagged via the Random Forest model) can be proactively expedited or
flagged to customers before they become complaints, rather than
discovered after the fact.
